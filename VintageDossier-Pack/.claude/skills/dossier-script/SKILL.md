# Writing for the Dossier style
## Topics (Stage 1)
Strong topics have: a date, a place you can show on a map, named people (photos exist), a turning point, and a
question the viewer wants answered. Title formulas: "The [Day/Hour] That [Consequence]", "How [Small Event] Started
[Big War]", "The [Crime] Nobody Solved", "Why [Empire] Really [Fell/Left]". South-Asia angles (British Raj, Partition,
1971, Karachi/Lahore/Bombay incidents, 80s-90s cases) perform well for this audience. Check the ledger to avoid repeats.

## Research (Stage 2)
Search pattern: `<topic> timeline`, `<topic> causes`, `<key person> biography`, `<topic> casualties`, `<topic> map`,
`<topic> controversy`. Prefer britannica.com, history.com, bbc.co.uk, ushmm.org, nationalarchives.gov.uk, loc.gov,
dawn.com, thehindu.com, university sites, Wikipedia (cross-check). Record lon/lat for places. Flag disagreements.

## Script (Stage 4)
- 150 words/min. Cold open (≤ 10 s): drop into the most dramatic moment, then "But to understand why, we have to go back…".
- Sentences of 6-16 words. One idea per sentence. Concrete nouns: dates, places, names, numbers.
- Every 20-30 s give the eye a "visual hook" word: a date (→ date card), a place (→ map), a person (→ print),
  a number (→ big number card), a document (→ telegram print), a verdict (→ stamp).
- Chapter headers (`## Chapter 2 - The Spark`) every 60-120 s. Do not read headers aloud.
- Neutral, factual tone; show both sides on disputed facts; no graphic gore; respectful with victims.
- Ending: answer the opening question + one memorable line. No "like and subscribe" unless the viewer asks.
- `script_spoken.txt`: same words, numbers/symbols spelled out, abbreviations expanded the way they are read.

## Shot & asset list (Stage 5)
For each beat: `mm:ss (est) | words | visual | source A/B/C`.
B-items (web): code-block search query + where to search + filename. Good queries are specific:
```
Neville Chamberlain 1939 portrait photograph
```
```
Westerplatte 1 September 1939 photograph Wikimedia Commons
```
C-items (AI) prompt template (copy-paste, in a code block):
```
Authentic 1930s black-and-white press photograph of [scene], grainy film stock, soft focus, light scratches and dust,
documentary realism, natural light, muted contrast, no text, no watermark, no modern objects, 3:2 aspect ratio
```
Era variants: 1940s war-time newsreel still / 1970s faded colour Kodachrome / 1980s 35mm colour press photo /
1990s VHS-era news photo. Never generate identifiable real people or fake a specific real event; AI is for atmosphere.
Targets: ~1 image per 15-25 s of video. Tell the viewer the total count and a zip-naming rule.
